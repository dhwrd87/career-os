# Make learning part of your career plan

Learning is most useful when it helps you do something that matters for the work you want. Career OS starts with a job's requirements and your current experience. It looks for the smallest useful next step, rather than a long list of courses.

Ask, “Help me reach this stretch role,” or “Make a plan for my dream job.” Choose a current posting that represents the role. The assistant can help find one. Your plan will use that role's gaps, not gaps from unrelated jobs.

## Know what is missing

A gap may be knowledge, a practical skill, a credential, professional experience or a clear example of work you already do. These need different responses. A missing detail may simply need checking. A required degree or license needs the actual award; a short course does not replace it.

## Choose learning that fits

Tell the assistant what you already know, how you like to learn, the time you have each week, your budget and any subscriptions you own. These details help it avoid repeated introductory material and unaffordable options.

It can compare courses, reading resources, hands-on practice, certificate programs and exam preparation. Provider names and affiliate links do not decide the ranking. An account with a provider does not prove that every course is included in your subscription.

Current prices, starting levels, time needed and entry rules must come from checked sources. The public reader can find resources on supported pages and indexes. Authenticated course-provider search is not implemented. When facts are missing or old, the plan says what needs checking instead of making up an offer. Current retrieval also does not prove that old teaching material is up to date.

## Make something useful

A course can introduce ideas. A project lets you practise them and create work you can discuss. Keep your files and notes, test the result and ask someone suitable to review it. Use available tools and check any extra project costs before spending money.

For experience gaps, look for supervised work with the relevant responsibility. Be clear about whether it is employment, volunteering, study or a practice project. Training does not create professional experience.

## Track progress and review your profile

Record when you start and finish a step. Completed work can be checked using the actual files and human review. A changed file needs checking again. A course certificate and an industry certification are different; exam preparation does not award either one automatically.

When you have something new to show, review the proposed skill or actual award. Your approval is needed before it changes the saved career profile. Then compare the target role again and review the relevant resume and interview examples.

## Read the plan fairly

The plan compares likely learning value, the award, job relevance and work you could show. These are guides for a decision, not hiring or pay predictions. Time estimates and resource details can change. Old plans are marked for review; update them before acting.

Your workspace has a learning page, a text version and a PDF. Plans for individual roles also keep their own learning PDF. The [fictional example](../examples/workspace/README.md) shows actual generated outputs, not real course offers.
